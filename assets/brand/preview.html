golem — logo concepts

A golem is a creature of clay brought to life by an inscribed word, built from parts, and unfailingly literal in obeying its creator — the same promises this framework makes: compose typed parts, run, get a dependable result. The word golem also carries go in its first two letters. Every concept pairs carved stone with Go cyan: clay as the dependable body, cyan as the word that animates it.

Concept A · Primary mark candidate

The Golem Head

A friendly, blocky face assembled from three clay slabs — brow, eyes, jaw — with bolts at the ears. The cyan stroke on the forehead is the inscription that brings it to life; the cyan eyes are where the model looks back at you. Warm enough for a docs-site mascot, sturdy enough for an avatar, and it stays legible down to a 16 px favicon (three slabs, two cyan eyes, one cyan stroke).

Concept A · Smooth structure variations

Same clay, smoother structure

The palette and the story stay exactly as they are; only the structure changes. Four progressively smoother takes on the golem — from a single river-worn pebble to a head drawn with one continuous line of clay.

V1 · The Pebble

One monolithic river stone — a single smooth form, soft ear pebbles tucked behind, round cyan eyes. The smoothest mascot: nothing to align, nothing to break at small sizes.

V2 · The Cairn

The three slabs of Concept A melted into each other — a cairn of river stones whose seams are smooth curves instead of hard gaps. Keeps the layered stone ramp, loses every sharp edge.

V3 · The One-Line Golem

The head as a single continuous loop of clay — one smooth rope, round joins only. The most minimal friendly face; scales beautifully and animates trivially (the loop can draw itself in an intro).

V4 · The Ribbon G

Concept D with every edge eased: the carved G becomes one smooth ribbon of clay, and the cyan arm glides into the bowl on a curve instead of a sharp corner.

Concept B · Lockup

The “go” Wordmark

The name does half the work: go sits inside golem. Setting the first two letters in Go cyan makes the wordmark say “Go-native agent framework” without adding a single shape. Paired with the head mark it becomes the README and docs-site header. (Wordmark shown in a system rounded sans for the concept; the chosen direction gets a licensed or hand-drawn chunky geometric cut, outlined for the final asset.)

Text-only · Wordmarks

golem, set in clay

A standalone G mark will always borrow Google's shadow, so these drop the symbol entirely and hand-letter the name: every letterform is drawn from the same thick, round-capped strokes as the one-line golem — the wordmark is the clay. No font files needed; these are paths, not text. And the e is a little golem itself: a stone bowl for a head, the crossbar a mouth, and two cyan eyes set into its brow.

T1 · The "go" split — chosen as the official logo

The name carries its platform with it: go set in Go cyan, lem in ink. The framework's whole pitch in five letters.

T2 · The inscription l

All ink, except the l: it becomes the cyan inscription, rising taller than the word — the pillar that animates it, standing inside the name.

T3 · The eye o

All ink, and the o becomes the golem's eye — a stone ring with a cyan pupil. The agent is literally looking out of its own name.

Concept C · Monogram

The Brick G

A “G” assembled from clay blocks — composition made literal, like the framework’s typed tools stacking into an agent. Exactly one block is cyan: the arm of the G, the piece that completes the letter, the word that brings the golem to life. The most app-icon-ready direction; it works as a GitHub avatar, a CLI splash glyph, or a terminal-style block cursor companion.

Concept D · Minimal mark

The Inscription

The most restrained take: a clay tablet, a G carved in a single stroke, and the carving glowing cyan where the word enters it. It says “golem” and “Go” in one glyph with zero cuteness — the premium/editorial direction, closer to a type foundry mark than a mascot. Strongest at large sizes; the arm-to-arc relationship needs the 32 px-and-up range to stay crisp.
